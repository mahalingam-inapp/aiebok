## What you will learn

Identify a claim that needs independent verification.

## Check the detail a parent would act on

The draft says the building reopens tomorrow morning. It reads naturally and has the right school name. The head teacher's message says the next announcement will be tomorrow morning.

Read the two sentences side by side. An update time became a reopening time. Restore the distinction and give families the official update channel. One uncertain detail can matter more than the quality of the rest of the message.

### Read the notice as a parent

| Draft | What a reader could conclude |
|---|---|
| The school will reopen at 9 a.m. tomorrow. | It is safe to send my child to school at 9. |
| The building is closed today. The school will post another update tomorrow at 9 a.m. | Check the official channel at 9 before making a plan. |

The revisions may look small on a screen. They create different plans for a parent arranging childcare or travel. When you review an assisted draft, mark the sentences that tell someone what will happen or what they should do. Trace those sentences back to the source before editing for tone.

Try another version of the exercise. The school moves only one class online. Which words prevent a parent of a different class from mistakenly staying home?

## How it works

Language models generate plausible continuations based on learned patterns and supplied context. A fluent answer can include unsupported facts, outdated details, or an invented source. A link or citation is useful only when it actually supports the claim.

### Separate a claim from the wording around it

A model can produce a likely continuation of a sentence without checking the world. If its input says there will be an update tomorrow, it may fill in the more familiar idea that the school reopens tomorrow. The same behavior helps it write smoothly in routine cases. Fluency therefore provides little evidence that a particular detail is true.

Underline what a reader might act on. Dates, locations, amounts, rules, and commitments are often more important than a stylistic problem. Compare each claim with the source and ask whether the source is both current and authoritative for that decision. A link should support the exact claim in the draft. A link to a general school page does not establish a reopening time.

When a source is incomplete, keep that gap visible. “We will share another update at 9 a.m.” tells families what to do without implying that classes resume at 9. Try looking for uncertainty that changed form: pending became confirmed, estimate became promise, or possibility became instruction. Those transformations often matter more than grammar.

## Worked example

A tool says expenses must be filed within 45 days. The current dated policy says 30 days. Reading the policy resolves the discrepancy.

## Try it yourself

A generated meeting summary says a decision was unanimous. What would you check before reporting that detail?

??? success "Compare your answer"
    Check the notes or recording for the actual votes. If the record is incomplete, report that the decision was recorded and omit the claim about unanimity.
