## What you will learn

Distinguish prediction, generation, and rules in a familiar workflow.

## A draft and a decision are different jobs

Amal can ask a tool to simplify the closure notice or ask it what the district will decide tomorrow. Only one of those requests takes a confirmed decision as an input.

Rewriting confirmed information can save time because Amal can compare the draft with the original. A model's prediction about reopening cannot substitute for a decision by the school. Look for who has access to the relevant facts and who has authority to act.

## How it works

A rule applies a condition someone specified. A predictive model estimates a label or value from patterns. A generative model produces content, such as text or an image, from a request and the information it receives. Several of these can coexist in one workflow.

### Start with the operation the tool performs

Imagine three buttons in a school office. One sends a closure notice to every parent on a list. Another estimates how many calls the office might receive. A third drafts an answer to a parent’s question. The first follows a rule, the second estimates an unknown quantity, and the third generates text. All three can be useful in one workflow, but each can fail in a different way.

A rule works only as well as the condition and data it receives. A prediction needs examples or measurements that have some relation to the future. A generated draft may sound right even when the tool had no access to the fact it describes. Classify the job before choosing the tool so you know what to check.

When a school worker uses a model to simplify a confirmed notice, the original notice remains the evidence. Compare the dates and instructions in both versions. If the job changes to estimating when a repair will finish, a draft about the repair cannot stand in for a prediction from the facilities team. In your own workflow, name the source, the operation, and the person who ultimately makes the decision.

## Worked example

A help desk uses a rule to acknowledge every ticket, predicts which team should own it, and drafts a response with a generative model. The support employee still checks that the response answers the right question.

## Try it yourself

Classify three tasks as rules, predictions, or generation. Warn when a field is empty, estimate when a parcel will arrive, and draft an update to the customer.

??? success "Compare your answer"
    The missing-field warning is a rule, the arrival estimate is prediction, and the draft update is generation.
