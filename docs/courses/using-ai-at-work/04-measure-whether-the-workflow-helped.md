## What you will learn

Compare the new workflow with the current workflow using a fair baseline.

## Count the time your colleague spent

A manual announcement takes 14 minutes to write and 4 to review. The tool drafts it in 3 minutes but the reviewer needs 11 minutes to check it.

The draft workflow totals 14 minutes rather than 18, a saving of four minutes in this example. Compare material corrections too. Time saved in drafting alone would make the change look much larger and miss the shift in who did the work.

### Compare a week of work

Suppose Maya and her colleague try two drafts using each workflow. Assume the draft and review times below cover every attempt, including the one that needs a correction.

| Method | Drafting minutes | Review minutes | Material corrections |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Manual | 28 | 8 | 1 |
| Assisted | 6 | 22 | 2 |

Across this tiny sample, drafting time dropped by 22 minutes, but review time grew by 14. The observed saving is eight minutes in total. The assisted version also required one more material correction. A single overall score would hide the trade: Maya saves time while her colleague takes on more review work.

Before extending the trial, Maya asks whether that reviewer is available near the publication deadline. If not, the four-minute saving per notice may make little difference to whether announcements go out on time. Write one observable condition that would make the workflow worthwhile for both people.

## How it works

A baseline records what happens today for comparable work. Count time spent prompting, checking, and reworking alongside drafting time. Track outcome quality and time separately so a faster draft does not hide larger review costs.

### Compare total work rather than draft speed

Time a request from a usable brief through an accepted final result. Include source collection, review, corrections, and any abandoned attempts. Use the same quality checks for manual and assisted work. Otherwise the result can reflect how strict the reviewer was rather than any difference in the tools.

One short note might take four minutes to write and two to check manually. An assisted version takes one minute to generate and six to verify. Although the draft arrives faster, the final result takes a minute longer. Another task with a more repetitive format might show the opposite result. Compare work by task type before aggregating.

A pilot should also identify who does the work. A writer may save time while a specialist review queue grows. Track whether that queue causes missed deadlines or interruptions. Use a small trial to decide which tasks are worth keeping, and make it easy for staff to switch back when a case falls outside the tested scope.

## Worked example

If the old workflow takes 15 minutes and the new one takes 5 minutes to draft plus 12 to check, the new process takes 17 minutes for that example.

### Follow the example

**Given:** An assisted draft takes 5 minutes to create, 8 to review, and 4 to fix. The previous verified draft took 15 minutes.

**Walkthrough:** Compare 17 minutes with 15. Record corrections separately. Prefer the method that meets the deadline and quality requirement on comparable cases.

## Try it yourself

Plan a comparison for drafting five routine notices. What will you record for each method?
