## What you will learn

Write a brief with context, constraints, and a checkable output.

## Give the assistant the job you would give a colleague

Maya has confirmed the address, room number, and start time. An earlier message lists a side entrance, but the facilities team has not confirmed it.

Separate verified facts from pending details in the brief. Say who will read it, what the reader needs to do, and which uncertainty must stay visible. For the entrance, ask the draft to say accessibility directions will follow.

## How it works

A useful brief specifies who will read the output, the intended decision or action, which facts to use, and the shape of the response. Explicit unknowns give the reviewer a way to distinguish missing information from invented detail.

### A brief has a purpose and an acceptance test

Handing a task to a writing tool resembles briefing a colleague who has never seen the project. Describe the reader, the action they should take, the facts they may rely on, and the shape of the result. Distinguish required facts from tone preferences. A paragraph may be flexible in style while the date and address are fixed.

Try two briefs for the workshop notice. “Announce that we moved indoors” leaves the tool to infer the address and what attendees should do. “Write a short notice for attendees. Keep the confirmed room and start time unchanged. State that accessible entrance directions will follow” gives the draft a concrete acceptance test. Compare the results one requirement at a time.

Include contradictions when you expect them. If an old flyer and a newer update disagree, identify which supersedes the other. If you cannot establish that, instruct the draft to surface the disagreement for review. This is especially useful when several people hand off work and nobody remembers which version supplied a detail.

## Worked example

Instead of asking for a good office notice, specify its audience, the known closure date, a 100-word limit, and ask for a placeholder if the reopening date is unknown.

### Follow the example

**Given:** Someone writes ‘Tell everyone the workshop changed.’ The destination room is confirmed, the accessible entrance is still being checked.

**Walkthrough:** Brief the tool to write to registered attendees, retain the confirmed location, and identify the entrance as pending. Check those three requirements before judging the tone.

## Try it yourself

Write a three-sentence brief for an email about a rescheduled training session when the replacement date is unknown.

??? success "Compare your answer"
    Ask for a short email to enrolled staff. State the original date and the fact of postponement. Ask for a placeholder for the new date and omit speculation.
