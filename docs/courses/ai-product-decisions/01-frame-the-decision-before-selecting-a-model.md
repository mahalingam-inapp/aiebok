## What you will learn

Describe a business workflow in terms of its user, friction, and outcome.

## Name the wait before naming the technology

Callers asking for hours wait in the same queue as callers who need staff attention. A new app would be visible to sponsors, but it is unclear which part of the queue it would improve.

Observe one shift and count repeat questions, time to answer, and which requests staff redirect. Pick a bottleneck such as routine lookup, then compare ways to reduce it before allocating scope to AI.

## How it works

Start with who does the work, which decision they make, how often it occurs, and where the current process fails. Quantifying the baseline makes proposals comparable. A narrow problem gives a pilot a result you can attribute to the change.

### Trace the job from the user's point of view

A product request such as “add a chatbot” names an interface rather than the burden it removes. Follow a caller from the moment they need an answer to the moment their task is resolved. Note where they wait, where they repeat information, and where a staff member exercises judgment.

Suppose a caller waits six minutes to ask for opening hours. The staff answer takes 20 seconds. A clear, maintained schedule page may reduce the wait without requiring people to explain their question to a model. If many callers need to understand how two written policies interact, search and a staff handoff may address a different gap. The two cases need different tests.

Turn an observation into a narrow decision. State the audience, workflow step, measured baseline, and change you can verify. Ask which other paths users take when the new feature fails. A pilot focused on a real bottleneck is easier to stop, improve, or expand than one that promises to automate the entire service.

## Worked example

Instead of proposing an AI help desk, target the time to route incoming requests to the correct team. Measure routing time and how often requests are reassigned.

## Try it yourself

Reframe ‘We need a chatbot for onboarding’ into a user, a repeated problem, and a measurable outcome.

    New hires have trouble finding the correct benefits form. Measure how many find it without contacting HR and how long it takes.
